Build a tool that generates nested-shape colour-step images — the Homage to the
Square family: concentric flat rings stepping through a colour ramp, the
convergence point pulled off its axis so the image reads as a well. One HTML
file, Canvas2D, no dependencies. Everything below is the mechanism; the control
panel around it is yours.

THE RINGS

N rings (3–16), ring 0 filling the frame, each later ring smaller and on top.
There are two nesting models and they are not the same picture:

1. SCALE — every ring is a similar copy: half-extents = frame half-extents × s(i).
   This is the Albers model; rings keep the frame's proportions.
2. INSET — equal bands: subtract the same distance d(i) from both half-extents.
   This is what the classic nested-rectangle posters actually do — check one:
   band widths stay ~constant in pixels while the rings shrink, so on a portrait
   frame the innermost rectangle goes tall and thin. A scaled copy cannot do that.

Ease the progression: with u = i/(N-1), use e = u^pace. pace > 1 keeps the first
bands thin (the reference frame measures ≈ 1.15); pace < 1 widens them inward.
s(i) runs 1 → inner (the innermost ring's relative size, ~0.05–0.6).

THE DRIFT

The well converges somewhere; that somewhere is a parameter, not the centre.
Per ring, per axis: offset = drift × e × slack, where slack = frame half-extent
minus ring half-extent. Ring 0 has no slack and never moves; the innermost ring
moves furthest. Drag on the canvas should write drift directly (double-click to
recentre). Seeded per-ring jitter adds wobble on the same slack.

THE SHAPES

Rounded rect, ellipse, hexagon, triangle, diamond. Build each ring as a Path2D
in local coordinates scaled anisotropically to the ring box — the hexagon is
(hw·cos, hh·sin) at 90°+k·60°, not a regular polygon — then addPath it through a
DOMMatrix translate+rotate into world space. One world-space path per ring means
fill and clip share the same geometry. Twist = per-step rotation in degrees.

CONTAINMENT

With drift, non-rectangular rings can honestly cross their parent's edge:
slack is computed on the bounding box, and a triangle does not fill its box.
Offer both behaviours. Free: let them overlap (paper-collage look). Clip: after
filling ring i, ctx.clip(path) — clips accumulate inside one save/restore, so
every later ring is confined to the intersection of all its ancestors and the
well nests strictly.

THE RAMP

Two or three colour stops, outer → (mid) → inner. Blend per-channel in RGB, or
convert to HSL and interpolate — but hue needs a path decision:
  short: dh = ((h2 − h1 + 540) % 360) − 180
  long:  dh − 360 if dh ≥ 0 else dh + 360   (the rainbow trick)
A grey endpoint has no hue of its own — borrow the other stop's hue or the ramp
sweeps through arbitrary colours on its way to grey. Apply a gamma curve to the
position before sampling; a direction flip is just t → 1−t. Worth knowing: the
classic blue nested-rectangle posters are HSL ramps, not RGB — the RGB midpoint
of the sampled endpoints misses the sampled middle ring, the HSL midpoint lands
on it. Preview the ramp in the UI by sampling the real blend function, not with
a CSS gradient, or the HSL paths will lie.

LIGHT AND MOTION

Sheen: per-ring linear gradient from the ring colour lightened to darkened,
along an angle fixed to the page (compute the gradient line in world space).
Shadow: canvas shadowBlur + directional offsets per ring, skipping ring 0 —
stacked paper. Grain: a small seeded grey-noise tile drawn as a repeating
pattern with composite 'overlay'. Flow: animate the colour position with a
triangle wave, pos = pingpong(t + phase) where pingpong(x) folds x into [0,1] —
at phase 0 it is the identity, so the still image and the first animation frame
agree exactly. Honor prefers-reduced-motion by zeroing flow.

EXPORT AND RANDOM

Export renders the same paint function into an offscreen canvas at exact chosen
pixels (an aspect ratio × a long-edge size), never by scaling the screen canvas.
Randomize twice, differently: Dice rerolls only the seed (jitter and grain).
Shuffle deals a curated look — pick a palette strategy (mono dark→light of one
hue; duo across the wheel; ember black→saturated→pale through a mid stop; pale
light→deep), build the stops in HSL with an enforced lightness gap so contrast
survives, weight rectangles over the other shapes, and make twist, sheen,
shadow, and jitter occasional rather than constant. Uniform randomness across
all parameters produces mud; curation is the feature.
