Build a tool that takes any picture and redraws it as a field of marks: a grid
where every cell's tone picks ONE glyph in ONE colour. Bars that line up join
into long strokes. The result should read as the picture from across the room
and as pure pattern up close.

THE PIPELINE

1. Sample the picture onto a grid. The person sets the number of columns;
   "cell height" is the cell's height ÷ width (1 = square), and
   rows = ceil(frameHeight / (frameWidth / columns × cellHeight)). Downscale in halving steps
   before the final draw into a cols × rows canvas, so each cell averages its
   area instead of aliasing a single pixel. Place the picture with cover or
   contain, then zoom and pan. Cells the picture does not reach are empty.

2. Tone per cell: lum = 0.299 r + 0.587 g + 0.114 b. Then LEVELS, then
   contrast about the middle — clamp((v − 0.5) · contrast + 0.5) — then gamma,
   invert, and a brightness trim.

   Levels is the step everyone skips and then wonders why their photo came
   back blank. Fixed tier edges only work on pictures graded for them; a normal
   phone photo sits in the bottom third of the range and lands entirely in the
   darkest tier. Offer three:
   - fixed: tones as they are;
   - auto: stretch the picture's own 2nd–98th percentile to 0..1;
   - equalise: replace each tone with its rank among all cells (mid-rank for
     runs of equal tones), so every tier covers the same share of the frame.
   Measure the percentiles once per grid, not per frame, and only over cells
   the picture actually covers.

3. Tiers: tier = floor(tone · N), N between 2 and 6, even edges. Default:
   N = 5 with tier 0 empty. Every tier has
   its own glyph, and tiers run dark to light. Making the darkest tier "empty"
   gives the classic look: shadows drop out, the light becomes structure.

4. Weight per tier, as a share of the cell. Either flat (every tier the same)
   or a ramp that thickens toward the light: the first inked tier gets a
   minimum weight, the last gets 1, a power curve between, all times a global
   scale: w = (min + (1 − min) · t^curve) · scale, where t runs 0..1 across the
   INKED tiers only. With N = 5 and tier 0 empty, the default ramp is widths
   1/8, 1/2, 3/4, 1 of a cell times 0.66 (min 0.125, curve ≈ 0.77). Weight is a
   share of the cell's width for columns and marks, of its height for rows.
   Palette colours are assigned from the first inked (darkest) tier upward, so
   the first colour always lands on the thinnest bars — pick palettes knowing
   that.

THE GLYPHS

column, row, flow, solid, square, ring, dot, diamond, checker, cross, slash,
empty. List "empty" LAST in any menu — it is the choice people make least.

- column / row: a bar of the tier's weight, centred across the cell and running
  its full length along the axis.
- RUNS: walk each column (for columns) or row (for rows); consecutive cells of
  the same tier and the same axis become ONE bar. Where a run ends, inset it by
  half the gap — the full gap is 4.5% of a cell, so each end moves in 2.25% —
  and round the end with a cap radius of roundness × half its width (≈ 44%).
  In picture-colour mode a run takes the colour of its first cell (top or
  left). This joining is the whole look. Without
  it you get a grid of stubs; with it you get woven strokes. Make joining a
  switch so people can see the difference.
- flow: a bar that turns to follow the picture. Compute Sobel gradients at
  cell scale on the RAW cell luminance (before levels and contrast), build the
  structure tensor (gx², gy², gx·gy) and box-blur it with radius 3 cells (a
  7-cell window, separable) so an edge's direction reaches into the flat area
  beside it. With E = Bxx + Byy: if E > 0.004 and |Bxx − Byy| > 0.25 · E, the
  tensor decides — energy mostly in x means the edge runs vertically, so the
  bar goes down it; mostly in y, across it. Otherwise decide with a seeded
  patchwork of 6 × 6-cell blocks (hash of block x, block y and the seed; below
  0.5 → down, else across). Without the patchwork flat regions all default to
  one axis and the maze never appears. Flow cells join into runs exactly like
  columns and rows, along whichever axis they chose.
- square, ring, dot, diamond, cross, slash: one mark per cell, size = weight ×
  the cell's short side. Ring, cross and slash are strokes about 18% of the
  mark (stroke = max(0.6 px, 0.18 × mark size), drawn inside the mark's box).
  Slash alternates direction on a checker parity.
- checker: inks only cells where (x + y) is even, as a rectangle of weight ×
  the cell.
- solid: fills the cell exactly and ignores weight, so neighbours fuse into
  flat areas.

COLOUR

A palette of up to six colours assigned from the first inked tier upward and
repeating if it runs short, OR each cell's own colour from the picture. One
ground colour. An optional underlay draws the picture itself, faintly, beneath
the marks.

MOTION (every mode must close on its loop)

- reveal: the picture is replaced by the field cell by cell. Orders: sweep
  (column by column, left to right), rows (top to bottom), radial (from the
  centre), scatter (seeded). Start and end the loop
  on the field, ease back to the picture at the half (smoothstep of
  |2·phase − 1|). A frame exported at time 0 is then the treatment, not the
  photo. Unrevealed cells show the picture through a clip.
- cycle: tone = fract(min(tone, 0.99999) + phase). Apply it at every phase —
  including 0 — or a tone of exactly 1 jumps a tier where the loop wraps.
- drift: tone += 0.18 · amount · sin(2π · phase).

THE POINTER

Keep a trail of recent pointer positions that fades over a second or so. Modes:
light and dark add or subtract tone under the brush (each trail point pushes
by weight × (1 − smoothstep(0, brush, distance))), and reveal shows the field
only under the pointer. The pointer is input, never saved state, and exports
ignore it.

ONE SCENE, THREE OUTPUTS

Build a plain list of shapes (rect, rounded bar, ring, dot, diamond, cross,
slash — each with position, size and colour) and draw it three ways: the
canvas on screen, a PNG at any size, and an SVG. Because all three come from
the same list, the vector export is exactly the picture on screen. Rounded bars
become rect with rx. That SVG is what makes this a print tool, not a filter.

THE PICTURE

Let people drop, paste or choose a picture. Keep a downscaled copy (long edge
~1600 px, JPEG) in local storage so a reload keeps it; a shared link carries
the settings, never the picture. Ship a few procedural samples so the tool is
never empty.

CONTROLS WORTH HAVING

columns, cell height; tiers and a glyph per tier; weight (ramp/flat), scale,
lightest, curve, join bars, gap, caps; colour mode, palette, ground, underlay;
levels, contrast, gamma, invert, brightness; fit, zoom, pan; motion, order,
amount, loop; pointer, brush, strength, trail; frame aspect (including "match
the picture"); a seed; and presets.

Useful presets: a warm maze (one tier of flow bars in black on a yellow ground,
the light empty); blue columns on black (thin dark blue in the shadows, wide
light blue in the light); a figure (checker, white rows, orange columns on
near-black); icons (rings, squares and dots in five saturated colours on
black).
